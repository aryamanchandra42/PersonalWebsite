                  Assumptions

                  How a style becomes rates

                  There is no kushti, kalari, or silambam bout file in this project. The measured numbers are UFCStats, frozen before 1 January 2025. A discipline contributes a foundation. The rest of the card is the full rate vector of UFC fighters who started in that same kind of base and then learned the other arts. Jon Jones was a wrestler first; he also had boxing and jiu-jitsu. That is the pattern. He is not the template.

                  Three kinds of number

                  Measured. UFC per-minute rates and the holdout on this page. Computed from fights before 2025.

                  Inferred. A discipline mapped onto a UFC cohort. Written in the roster file, not fitted to Indian results.

                  Hypothetical. A slider. The chain is re-run with one rate edited. That is a what-if, not a claim the athlete will gain the skill.

                  The four UFC cohorts

                  Inside each division, with at least eight minutes of cage time:

                  • Wrestler-first: top quarter of takedowns and of time on top. The card keeps their striking and submissions.
                  • Striker-first: top quarter of standing strikes and knockdowns, with their real wrestling attached.
                  • Judoka-like: top quarter of clinch share and takedowns.
                  • Range-first: top quarter of time at distance and a low clinch share.

                  If a division cohort has fewer than four fighters, the model falls back to the men’s or women’s pool.

                  The map

                  Kushti and freestyle use the wrestler-first card. Kushti pins set top-exit to that cohort’s low end. Judo uses the throw-first card. Boxing uses striker-first hands and that cohort’s grappling as camp. Kickboxing uses the full striker-first card. Kalaripayattu empty hand is a shrunk striker card plus a small lock step for submissions. Silambam keeps only distance; the stick is not a jab. Named athletes contribute weight, sex, age, and a source URL. No medals are typed in as rates.

                  The model

                  The mathematics

                  Every fighter is a vector of per-minute rates, shrunk toward the league. A matchup is a continuous-time Markov chain on eight states, stepped every five seconds. What follows is the model that is actually running above, not a sketch of a later one.

                  1. States

                  The chain lives on eight states. The first four are transient; the last four are absorbing finishes.

                  \[ \mathcal{S} = \{\mathrm{DIST},\;\mathrm{CLINCH},\;A_{\mathrm{top}},\;B_{\mathrm{top}},\;A_{\mathrm{KO}},\;A_{\mathrm{SUB}},\;B_{\mathrm{KO}},\;B_{\mathrm{SUB}}\}. \]

                  A fight that lasts the distance is the event that the chain is still in a transient state after the scheduled rounds. Each new round restarts the remaining probability mass at distance: fatigue is not a separate clock, because durability already shows up in who gets finished late.

                  2. Estimating a fighter

                  From UFCStats round rows we accumulate counts \(c\) and exposure \(t\) (minutes in the relevant position). The league rate is the pooled ratio \(\lambda = C/T\). Each fighter’s rate is an empirical-Bayes shrink toward that league value, with prior weight \(m_0\) minutes:

                  \[ \hat\lambda = \frac{c + \lambda\, m_0}{t + m_0}. \]

                  Standing actions use \(m_0 = 15\) minutes (\(30\) for knockouts). Top and bottom use \(m_0 = 6\) (\(12\) for ground finishes). Every rate comes in an offensive version (what the fighter does) and a vulnerability version (what they concede).

                  The rates on each fighter are: takedowns, clinch share, top exit, bottom exit, sweeps, standing KO, ground-and-pound KO, submissions, and strikes landed or absorbed standing and on the ground.

                  3. Combining two fighters

                  A matchup is not the average of two careers. If \(A\) takes people down at \(\lambda^{\mathrm{off}}\) and \(B\) is taken down at \(\lambda^{\mathrm{vuln}}\), against a league base \(\lambda\), the pairing uses a log5-style product:

                  \[ \lambda_{A\to B} = \frac{\lambda^{\mathrm{off}}_A \cdot \lambda^{\mathrm{vuln}}_B}{\lambda}. \]

                  A wrestler against someone who never wrestles stays a wrestler. A puncher against a glass jaw is more dangerous than either rate alone.

                  4. The rate matrix

                  \(Q\) is an \(8\times 8\) matrix of per-minute transition rates. Standing (distance and clinch) share the same takedown and standing-KO rates. The clinch is a two-state exchange with a fixed exit of \(2.4\) per minute — about twenty-five seconds — and an entry rate that matches the pair’s clinch share \(s\):

                  \[ Q_{\mathrm{DIST},\mathrm{CLINCH}} = 2.4\cdot\frac{s}{1-s},\qquad Q_{\mathrm{CLINCH},\mathrm{DIST}} = 2.4,\qquad s = \min\bigl(0.9,\;\tfrac12(s_A+s_B)\bigr). \]

                  On the ground the exit rate is the product of the top man’s escape-allowing rate and the bottom man’s stand-up rate. A sweep is that exit, capped at \(80\%\) of it; the rest is a return to distance. Ground KO and top-side submission are scaled by a global calibration \(c_{\mathrm{g}}\). Submissions off the bottom are a small constant, \(0.004\) per minute.

                  Standing and ground KO rates are multiplied by fitted scales \(c_{\mathrm{KO}}\) and \(c_{\mathrm{g}}\) so that predicted finish rates match the last \(1{,}500\) training fights.

                  5. Competing hazards, five seconds at a time

                  From state \(i\), the holding time is exponential with hazard \(h_i = \sum_j Q_{ij}\). In a step of \(\Delta t = 5/60\) minutes the exact transition probabilities are

                  \[ P_{ii} = e^{-h_i\Delta t},\qquad P_{ij} = \bigl(1-e^{-h_i\Delta t}\bigr)\frac{Q_{ij}}{h_i}\quad(j\neq i). \]

                  Absorbing rows are the identity. This is not an Euler discretisation of \(Q\); it is the one-step law of competing exponential clocks.

                  6. Forward propagation

                  Let \(p^{(0)} = e_{\mathrm{DIST}}\). Each five-second step is \(p \leftarrow pP\). After \(60\) steps a round ends. Any mass still in the transient block is lifted back to distance for the next round. Occupancy in position \(k\) is the expected minutes there:

                  \[ \mathrm{occ}_k = \sum_{\text{steps } t} p_t(k)\,\Delta t. \]

                  The finish vector \(f = (f_{A_{\mathrm{KO}}}, f_{A_{\mathrm{SUB}}}, f_{B_{\mathrm{KO}}}, f_{B_{\mathrm{SUB}}})\) is the absorbing mass at the final horn. The decision probability is \(d = 1-\sum f\).

                  7. Who wins a decision

                  If the fight reaches the judges, the winner is a logistic in two differentials: expected strikes per minute and expected control (top minutes minus bottom minutes), both using the same product combination as the chain:

                  \[ z = \beta_1\cdot\frac{\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{land}_A-\mathrm{land}_B]}{\mathrm{occ}} + \beta_2\cdot\frac{\mathrm{occ}_{A_{\mathrm{top}}}-\mathrm{occ}_{B_{\mathrm{top}}}}{\mathrm{occ}}, \qquad \mathbb{P}(A\text{ wins the cards}) = \sigma(z) = \frac{1}{1+e^{-z}}. \]

                  \(\beta\) is fitted by Newton’s method on real decision bouts: recorded strike and control differentials against who actually got the nod. The raw chain win chance is then

                  \[ p_{\mathrm{raw}} = f_{A_{\mathrm{KO}}} + f_{A_{\mathrm{SUB}}} + d\cdot\sigma(z). \]

                  8. Calibration on unseen fights

                  The chain alone is slightly overconfident. On a validation window the profiles never saw (2023–2024, built only from fights before 2023) we fit a single shrink \(\alpha\) on the logit:

                  \[ p = \sigma\bigl(\alpha\cdot\mathrm{logit}(p_{\mathrm{raw}})\bigr), \qquad \mathrm{logit}(p)=\log\frac{p}{1-p}. \]

                  The fitted \(\alpha\) is about \(0.49\): the shape of the chain is kept, the certainty is pulled toward a coin. Profiles for the public model are then rebuilt on all history. The published track record freezes those profiles at 1 January 2025 and scores every later fight blind.

                  9. Chances from here

                  The live bar is not a restatement of \(p\). It is the probability, from the current state \(x\) at step \(t\), that \(A\) (resp. \(B\)) absorbs before the final horn. If \(V^{(T)}_y = 1\) on \(A\)’s finishes and \(0\) elsewhere, then

                  \[ V^{(t)}_x = \sum_y P_{xy}\,V^{(t+1)}_y, \]

                  with the same round-restart at distance. That is why a takedown moves the bar even when the clock has barely run.

                  10. Matchmaking

                  Inside each division we take the top fifteen fighters by Elo (\(K=32\)). Every pair that has not met in two years is scored

                  \[ \mathrm{score} = (\text{closeness})^{3/2}\,(0.45+\text{finish})\,(0.55+0.45\cdot\text{stakes}), \]

                  where closeness is \(1-|2p-1|\), finish is \(\sum f\), and stakes is how high the pair sits in that division’s Elo range. No name is used more than twice on the published card.
