The Mathematical Basis

A Formal Treatment of Semantic Distance in Vector Spaces

§ I. The Embedding Space

Let V denote our vocabulary of cardinality |V| ≈ 400,000. We define an embedding function:

Definition 1.1 (Word Embedding):
φ : V → ℝd, where d = 100

The embedding space (ℝd, ⟨·,·⟩) forms an inner product space with the standard Euclidean inner product. Each word w ∈ V maps to a vector φ(w) learned via the GloVe objective, which minimizes:

GloVe Objective Function:
J = Σi,j f(Xij)(wiTw̃j + bi + b̃j − log Xij)2

where Xij denotes the co-occurrence count, and f is a weighting function satisfying f(0) = 0 with f(x) → ∞ as x → 0+ bounded.

§ II. Cosine Similarity as Angular Distance

For vectors u, v ∈ ℝd \ {0}, the cosine similarity is the normalized inner product:

Definition 2.1 (Cosine Similarity):
sim(u, v) = ⟨u, v⟩ / (‖u‖₂ · ‖v‖₂) = cos(θu,v)

Proposition 2.2: sim(·,·) ∈ [−1, 1] with equality iff the vectors are parallel (sim = 1) or antiparallel (sim = −1).

Proof: By Cauchy-Schwarz, |⟨u, v⟩| ≤ ‖u‖₂ · ‖v‖₂, with equality iff u = αv for some α ∈ ℝ. Division by the norms yields the result. ∎

Remark: The angular distance θ = arccos(sim(u, v)) defines a proper metric on the projective space ℝPd−1.

§ III. The Dissimilarity Metric

We define semantic distance as a monotone transformation of cosine similarity:

Definition 3.1 (Semantic Distance):
d(u, v) = 1 − sim(u, v) ∈ [0, 2]

Theorem 3.2: The function d(·,·) satisfies:

  • (i) Non-negativity: d(u, v) ≥ 0
  • (ii) Identity: d(u, v) = 0 ⟺ u ∥ v (parallel vectors)
  • (iii) Symmetry: d(u, v) = d(v, u)

Note: d(·,·) violates the triangle inequality in general, hence is a dissimilarity measure rather than a true metric. However, √d(u,v) does satisfy metric axioms for unit vectors.

§ IV. The Optimization Problem

Let {w0, w1, ..., wn} be the word sequence. At turn t, the player solves:

Player's Objective:
wt* = arg maxw ∈ V d(φ(wt-1), φ(w))

Subject to w ∉ {w0, ..., wt-1} (no repetitions). The cumulative score:

Total Score:
S = Σt=1n d(φ(wt-1), φ(wt))

Computational Complexity: Naïve search is O(|V| · d) per turn. Approximate nearest neighbor structures (e.g., HNSW) reduce this to O(log |V|) with bounded approximation error.

§ V. Geometric Intuition

The embedding manifold exhibits rich algebraic structure. Linear operations preserve semantic relationships:

Semantic Composition (Mikolov et al., 2013):
φ(king) − φ(man) + φ(woman) ≈ φ(queen)
Strategic Lemma: Antonyms often exhibit high cosine similarity (small distance) due to distributional co-occurrence in similar contexts. Maximum distance is achieved by selecting words from orthogonal semantic subspaces—e.g., abstract vs. concrete, emotional vs. technical.

Corollary: The optimal strategy is not to find opposites, but to find semantically unrelated terms: words whose embedding vectors span maximally independent directions in ℝd.
